Reviews & notes

How new Custom SOS package requests are checked, and what happens with the result.
AI review
Review new Custom SOS package requests with Claude as they arrive. Applies only to requests that arrive while it’s on; anything already listed keeps the decision it arrived with. Any request can still be reviewed by hand with its Run AI review button.
Auto add internal note
After every AI review (automatic or by button), add it to the Zendesk ticket as an internal note. Agents only; the customer is not notified. If it fails, the request says why.

Translation

The Translate to English button on an SOS request.
Translate with
The engine behind a request’s Translate to English button. Claude: the best translations, a cent or two each. Spark: Splashtop’s own AI, free, a smaller model. A translation is made once per request and kept.

Import

Bring past SOS requests from Zendesk into SOS Scans. New ones arrive by themselves through the Zendesk webhook.
Past requests
Find every past “New SOS package created by…” ticket in Zendesk and list it on SOS Scans, its images shown from Zendesk. No AI review. Tickets already listed are skipped, and it carries on by itself after a restart.

Zendesk Tickets

Zendesk tickets since 1 January 2026, kept here so the AI page can find similar cases, topics and trends, and the Knowledge Base can count the tickets that link each article. Read-only: nothing is written to Zendesk.
2026 tickets
Each ticket’s subject, tags, status and dates; the requester’s e-mail, organization and a country guess; and the whole conversation, every reply and internal note marked Customer or Support. Support agents are never named: their names, Splashtop addresses and signatures are taken out. Attachments are not kept. SOS package and SSO validation tickets are left out (they have their own pages).
Two steps, both carrying on by themselves after a restart: first the tickets, a week at a time (quick); then each ticket’s conversation, newest first (a few hours for the year; searching works meanwhile). Once done, new and changed tickets are added every hour.

Zendesk Knowledge Base

Splashtop’s Zendesk Help Center articles, kept here for the Knowledge Base page and the AI page. Read-only: nothing is written to Zendesk.
Help Center articles
Every article in every language the Help Center has: title, text, section and category, link, labels, draft / agents-only / promoted / outdated, helpful votes, and dates. Not kept: authors, comments or images. A few hundred calls to Zendesk; once downloaded it syncs again every 6 hours, and drops articles Zendesk no longer has.

PO Requests

Zendesk tickets that carry a “Provision Details” order, from all years, for the PO Requests page and the Customers page. Read-only: nothing is written to Zendesk.
All PO requests
A Zendesk search for “Provision Details” over every year; each order is read the way SplashHub’s PO tool reads it (SPID, order type, expected provision date, region, products and quantities). Splashtop staff addresses are not kept. Once imported, new and changed ones are added every hour.

Teams notifications

Cards posted to a Teams channel when something needs a person. Each event is posted once; a card carries the ticket number, the package / domain / company and a button to open it here — never an agent’s name.

Language routing
For “Open RR tickets in a routed language”: each language and the Splashtop people to @mention. A ticket’s language comes from its Zendesk tag when it has one (optional here; Auto Tag adds language_xx), otherwise Spark reads the ticket and picks one of these languages or “other” — once per ticket. Spark

Model
Which of Spark’s models answers: the AI page, Spark translations, and the dashboard’s spike notes and lookalike names. Smaller models answer faster; bigger ones may answer better. Use Test speed to compare.
Let the model think first
Some models (like Qwen3) write a long hidden chain of reasoning before every answer. Off is much faster and fine for lookups, translations and tags; turn it on only if answers seem careless.

Price feed

Where the PriceBook gets its prices on splashtop.com.
Price feed
Where the Price Book reads its prices on splashtop.com. The link carries a version hash that changes whenever splashtop.com rebuilds; the Price Book re-finds it by itself, or scan here to do it on demand, or paste a link to set it by hand.
