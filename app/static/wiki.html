Getting started

What SplashHub Centre is

SplashHub Centre is the Splashtop support team’s web app, hosted on Spluki. It works next to SplashHub, the sidebar app inside Zendesk: SplashHub helps on the ticket you have open, Centre looks across all tickets — lists, dashboards, background checks and AI that runs by itself.

  • Where: splashhub-37268f-dev.tperd.splashtop.dev. It only opens inside the company network (office or VPN).
  • Zendesk: Centre reads Zendesk with its own account. It changes Zendesk only in the few places listed in What Centre changes in Zendesk, and almost always only when someone presses a button.
  • AI: two kinds. Spark is Splashtop’s own AI (free) and does most of the work. Claude does the SOS package image reviews (paid, a few cents each, shown on Logs).

The menu and moving around

  • Left menu: every page. The button next to the logo folds it into icons (remembered in your browser); on narrow windows it’s always icons.
  • Pop-up panels: most lists open an item in a panel over the page. ↑ / ↓ move to the previous / next item, Esc closes.
  • Last view: when you come back to a page, it shows what you saw last time (slightly faded) while fresh data loads, so nothing sits empty. Kept in your browser for 7 days.
  • Loading: a thin moving line under the top bar means the page is fetching data after a click or search; grey shimmering blocks are placeholders on a first visit.
  • Holiday themes: around holidays the top bar and the menu get a light shade, a garland hangs under the top bar (twinkling lights, lanterns, bunting, diyas...) and a greeting with a bobbing emoji appears; nothing moves for anyone whose computer asks for less motion (admins choose in Settings › Look › Theme). Text and numbers never change colour.

SOS Scans

Custom SOS package requests — the Zendesk tickets “New SOS package created by …” — checked for brand misuse (someone white-labelling Splashtop to pose as a bank, Microsoft, a government…).

How requests arrive

  • A Zendesk trigger sends each new request to Centre through a webhook the moment the ticket is created.
  • If Settings › Reviews & notes › AI review is on, Claude reviews it right away; otherwise it’s only listed. Reviews that fail are retried every 10 minutes.
  • Old requests can be brought in with Settings › Import › Past requests (listed, not reviewed).

Dashboard view

  • Tiles: last 24 hours and last 7 days (against the usual), Needs review still open, High risk still open, share of generic e-mails over 7 days.
  • Requests per day for 21 days, coloured by verdict, with the usual for that weekday.
  • Needs attention: flagged or unreviewed requests whose ticket is still open, oldest first.
  • Spike alert: the last 24 h / 7 days against the usual, and Spark’s note on what the burst has in common (same domains, trials, similar names, time of day).
  • Who’s asking (creator domains, new vs returning teams), Package mix (trial / paid), Lookalike names (Spark: package names imitating a known brand), AI spend for 7 days.

View Scans (the list)

  • Filter by verdict, search by ticket, creator or package. The Ticket status column is the Zendesk ticket’s own status, refreshed every minute for open tickets.
  • Click a request for its panel: package details (created, creator, type — trial in red — subscription, technicians), what end users see (caption, instructions), the images (shown straight from Zendesk; click to enlarge), Translate to English, View team on ACP, Manage SOS PKG on ACP, Download package, the AI review card, Run AI review / Re-review, Add as internal note, and who pressed Scan in SplashHub.

The verdicts and the team’s rules

VerdictMeans
VerifiedNo issue found.
Needs reviewA person should look.
High riskA concrete reason to suspect brand misuse.

On top of the AI’s own call:

  • A generic / free e-mail (gmail, outlook, qq.com…) is never Verified — at least Needs review, until the customer gives a reason.
  • Branding that doesn’t match the creator’s domain (images say “Vaultorio”, e-mail is @datamaas.com) is at least Needs review; the card says which image shows which brand. Platform names on download links (Windows, Mac, Android, iOS, Linux, Chrome OS) and Splashtop itself never count.
  • Splashtop branding beyond “Powered by Splashtop” (e.g. “SOS by Splashtop”) is High risk. A small “Powered by Splashtop” and Splashtop’s default title bar are fine.
  • Scam-sounding details (“Fraud Investigation”, “IRS”, “Account suspended”) are flagged.
  • When a rule is added, older reviews are re-checked at start-up.

The AI review and internal notes

  • Claude reads the package’s images and details, with the same prompt and format as SplashHub’s sidebar scan. Images are never stored — downloaded in memory only for the review. Each review’s cost shows on its card and on Logs.
  • Add as internal note posts the review to the ticket as an internal note (agents only). Settings › Reviews & notes › Auto add internal note does it after every review.
  • The note has: status with confidence, reason, review, image and package details, and quick links (Manage SOS PKG, View team on ACP, Download package).

SSO Requests

Requests to validate an SSO method: the customer must add a DNS TXT record proving they own their domain.

How requests are found

  • Every 15 minutes Centre searches Zendesk for tickets tagged single_sign-on__sso_ (SplashHub’s SSO tool adds it with Insert Reply), tickets with a splashtop-sso-challenge record anywhere in the conversation, or the old SSO form’s subject. Updated tickets are read again.
  • It reads the whole conversation the way SplashHub’s SSO tool does and keeps every record: a TXT record on splashtop-sso-challenge.<domain> (or a dated host like -20260520.<domain>) with a 32-character value. Several domains per ticket are fine (the list shows “+1”).
  • Add a ticket by number, or Import past SSO requests to find all of 2026.
  • Spark double-check: tickets on the list only because of the tag are read once by Spark. If it’s not really an SSO request (one user who can’t sign in, a mis-tagged invoice), it moves to Not SSO. Tickets with records or the SSO form always count.

DNS checks

  • Every hour, each waiting request with an open ticket is checked by itself (all its records). Check DNS checks one now; Check all waiting checks every waiting one.
  • When the customer replies that they’ve added the record, Centre checks right away instead of waiting for the hour.
  • A request is Verified when every record carries its value. Solved and closed tickets aren’t checked anymore.

The list

  • Ticket-status chips: Open tasks (default: everything not solved or closed), Open, Pending, On-hold, then Solved / closed and Not SSO.
  • Request-status chips: Any status, Waiting, Verified, Enabled, Not found yet, Not checked, Needs details, Check failed.
  • Under each status, a small line says where it stands (see SSO stages). Orange lines need a reply.

A request’s panel

  • Requested, requester, ticket and its status, organization, where it stands.
  • The record to look for (domain, TXT host, expected value) — editable if the ticket wasn’t clear — and All records in the ticket with each domain’s own result.
  • The DNS check card with the TXT records found, earlier checks, and Add as internal note (the result, per domain).
  • Not an SSO request / It is an SSO request: a person’s choice always wins over Spark.

PO Requests: JP POs and US / EMEA POs

Every Zendesk ticket carrying a “Provision Details” order (purchase / provisioning), from all years, read the way SplashHub’s PO tool reads it. Two menus: JP POs (the Japanese orders) and US / EMEA POs (everything else: US, EMEA and On-Prem, with the region filter to narrow it). The numbers, the list and the filters follow the menu.

  • Each order: SPID, company, order type (Renewal, Renewal Upgrade, Upsell / Expansion, New Business), expected provision date, region (US, EMEA, JP, On-Prem), products with quantities and start / end dates (Japanese orders: their “Japanese Product Name” lines), and the whole conversation.
  • Top: open orders, due in the next 7 days, overdue (expected date passed, ticket still open), the last 30 days.
  • Filters: status, region, upcoming / overdue, year; search an SPID, company, product or ticket number.
  • Updates: imported once in Settings › Database › PO Requests, then new and changed ones every hour.

Customers

Everything Centre knows about one customer on one page. Search an e-mail domain, an address, or a company name. A customer is a domain — or, for free e-mail providers, one address.

  • Shows: organizations and country, first and last contact, quick links (ACP, Zendesk search); their 2026 tickets (count, still open, per month, top tags and topics, latest); Custom SOS packages with verdicts; SSO requests; PO / provisioning requests; the people who wrote in.
  • AI summary: a short Spark briefing on the customer.
  • With no search, it lists the most active customers of the last 30 days. Splashtop’s own domain is never a customer. It uses data already downloaded; nothing new is read from Zendesk.

PriceBook

Knowledge Base

Splashtop’s Zendesk Help Center articles, in every language.

  • Search by words, title or article number; filter by language, category, Promoted / Outdated translation / Agents only / Drafts; sort by most linked in tickets, helpful votes, “not helpful” votes, or update date.
  • Top: articles, updated in the last 30 days, linked in 2026 tickets, outdated translations.
  • An article: helpful % (votes), how many 2026 tickets link it (by month, latest tickets), dates, languages (outdated ones marked), labels and its text, with Open in the Help Center. Zendesk doesn’t provide page views.
  • Updates: downloaded in Settings › Database › Zendesk Knowledge Base, then synced every 6 hours.

Tags

How many tickets carry each Zendesk tag, from the downloaded 2026 tickets — the same sets as SplashHub’s Tags page.

  • Sets: Topics (SplashHub’s list, with groups like “2FA & recovery”), Languages, All tags (the most used).
  • Period: today, 7 or 30 days, with the change against the period before.
  • Views: Share (donut) or Heatmap (8 weeks). The table sorts by any column; every count opens the matching Zendesk search.
  • Choose topics: up to 15 on screen; your choice is remembered in your browser.

AutoTag Spark

Spark reads the language of every new ticket in the background — SplashTags’ job, done for every ticket whether or not anyone opens it. The tag it would add is held on this page to be checked; nothing is written to Zendesk unless someone presses a tag button.

How it reads a ticket

  • Every 2 minutes, tickets created from the start day on (the day it was first switched on). Scan new tickets reads them now.
  • Email / web form: the subject and the customer’s first message (SplashTags’ rule: later replies drift to English). Agent comments are left out.
  • Live chat: waits for the chat transcript (it arrives when the chat ends; at most a day), then reads every line the visitor wrote — no agent lines, no chat boilerplate.
  • Messaging: every customer message, once there is one.
  • Always-Japanese requesters (e.g. splashtop@och.co.jp) are Japanese whatever the text.
  • Skipped and hidden: provisioning requests (skip phrase “Provision Details”) and phone calls (voice channel, “Call from / Voicemail from” subjects, voicemail / hangup tags).

Languages

Japanese, Korean, Chinese (Simplified), Chinese (Traditional), English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese — each with its language_xx tag — plus Other (language_other) for a real language not on the list. No text (empty, a signature only) gets no tag.

The page

  • Numbers: tickets read, would tag (by language), same as the Zendesk tag (%), differs, checked by the team (% right).
  • Filters: search, language, result, checked or not; chips Differs from the Zendesk tag / No language tag in Zendesk / Same as Zendesk.
  • A ticket’s panel: Spark’s answer and confidence, the ticket’s tag in Zendesk now, what Spark read, ✓ Right / ✗ Wrong (and the right language), Add tag / Change tag (replaces another language tag), Clear tag (two clicks).
  • For the checking phase: tick boxes and Add tags, Add tags to all on this page (with a confirmation and breakdown). A ticket whose tag someone cleared on purpose is never put back by a mass add.
  • Tag changes use Zendesk’s tags-only update: the status never changes and nobody needs to submit.

Ad/Spam Filter Spark

Spark reads every new ticket and decides whether it’s marketing or spam — someone selling SEO, backlinks, website traffic, ads or development to Splashtop — or a real request. Only the ones it calls ads are listed.

How it scans

  • Every 2 minutes, every ticket created in the last 3 days (never before the day before it was first switched on), whatever its status. Older ads still open get their status refreshed once a day.
  • Reads tickets the same way as AutoTag (first message; chats after their transcript; messaging in full).
  • Never called spam: phone calls, provisioning requests, and tickets with no real text. A chat asking about Splashtop is never spam.
  • Spark compares each ticket with the example ads in Settings › AutoTag › Ad/Spam Filter. Paste new spam there when one slips through.

The page

  • Views: Open tasks (default: New, Open, Pending, On-hold), Solved / closed, Marked wrong. Each ticket’s status follows Zendesk.
  • A ticket’s preview: subject, sender, status and tags (fresh from Zendesk), Spark’s reason, the whole ticket (every reply and note, oldest first), ✓ Right / ✗ Wrong, Open in Zendesk.

Silent close

In a ticket’s preview, confirmed twice (the box, then a tick “I checked it: this ticket is spam”, then Yes, silent close):

  1. Adds the silent_close tag.
  2. One ticket update: a private note “Silent-Close” (never sent to the customer), Product = Don’t Know, Issue Type = Other (both required to solve), status Solved.
  3. Reads the ticket back and shows each part ✓ or ✗.

The ticket counts as ✓ Right. The button stays as Silent close again… to retry (each retry adds another note). If the ticket had no assignee, Zendesk may assign it to Centre’s Zendesk account when solving.

AI Spark

Questions answered by Spark from Centre’s own data. Nothing asked there changes anything or reaches Zendesk.

  • Can answer about: 2026 support tickets (counts, trends, common problems, how they were fixed, similar cases, one ticket in full), SOS and SSO requests, PO requests, customers, Knowledge Base articles, plans and prices (quotes; 4+ licences can get a volume price), the run log, and how Centre works.
  • “Draft a reply for #123” reads that ticket fresh from Zendesk, looks at how similar cases were fixed and relevant articles, and writes a reply. Copy draft copies just the reply.
  • Each answer shows what it looked up and how long it took. It never discusses individual support agents.

Spark activity

Every decision Spark makes behind the scenes, kept 30 days.

  • Summary: decisions in the last 24 hours, average and slowest answer, failures; a table by area for 7 days (decisions, average, per ticket, slowest, changed something, failed).
  • Areas: SSO (is it an SSO request? / where it stands), AutoTag (language), Ad/Spam Filter, Teams language routing, SOS Scans (translation, dashboard notes), Customers (summary).
  • Filters: area, ticket number, result (changed something / no change / failed). Links from the SSO, AutoTag and Ad/Spam panels open it on that ticket.
  • Click a decision: a pop-up with the ticket on the left (status, tags, whole conversation; a switcher when Spark read several at once) and Spark’s judgement on the right (what Centre did, time, its answer, its reasoning, what it read, the question).

Spark, Splashtop’s AI

  • Free, hosted by Splashtop; the model is shown in Settings › Spark.
  • Used for: AutoTag, the Ad/Spam Filter, the SSO checks, Teams language routing, the AI page, Customers summaries, SOS dashboard notes and (optionally) translations.
  • Background decisions are held for checking — nothing Spark decides changes Zendesk by itself. The ✓ Right / ✗ Wrong buttons measure how reliable it is.
  • Every background decision is kept in Spark activity. If Spark doesn’t answer, the work waits and is retried on the next round.

Teams notifications

Centre posts cards to the support Teams channel (a Workflows webhook). Each kind is switched on in Settings › Notifications › Teams, and Run now sends one at once.

  • Language routing: open round-robin tickets by language — “Hi Mia and Ken, there are 3 Japanese tickets that require your assistance” with links. A table sets each language’s tags, the people to mention and on/off. Untagged tickets are read by Spark. Checked every 30 minutes.
  • SOS Needs review / High risk: when a review flags a request.
  • SSO verified: when a request’s records are found in DNS.
  • Spikes: unusual bursts of SOS requests (every 15 minutes).
  • Overdue POs: expected provision date passed, ticket still open (hourly; switching it on first marks the current ones as known).
  • Each alert is posted once.

SplashHub sidebar and Centre

  • Pressing Scan on an SOS package ticket in SplashHub shows Centre’s review in the sidebar’s usual format (“Review from SplashHub Centre”), so the AI runs once per ticket. Review again runs a fresh one.
  • The sidebar calls Centre from your browser, so it needs the company network. Otherwise, after a few seconds, the sidebar reviews the ticket itself.
  • Every press is recorded on the request and on Logs. SplashHub also sends a copy of every run it logs to Centre.

Data Centre keeps

  • Zendesk Tickets: 2026 tickets and conversations (agents’ names removed in conversations used by the AI).
  • Help Center articles, PO requests (all years), SOS / SSO requests, AutoTag and Ad/Spam results with the team’s checks.
  • Logs of AI runs; Spark activity for 30 days.
  • Not kept: SOS package images (shown from Zendesk, held in memory only for a review).
  • In your browser only: the last view of each page (7 days), menu folded or not, chosen Tags topics.

FAQ and troubleshooting

The page won’t open.
Centre only answers inside the company network — connect to the VPN.
SOS images don’t load.
They show straight from Zendesk: sign in to Zendesk in the same browser.
The sidebar ran its own scan instead of Centre’s.
Centre wasn’t reachable from your browser within a few seconds (usually no VPN).
An SSO ticket is missing from the list.
Check the Solved / closed and Not SSO chips, or add it by number. It needs the SSO tag, a challenge record, or the SSO form subject to be found by itself.
Spark got something wrong.
Mark it ✗ Wrong (AutoTag, Ad/Spam) or use the Not SSO buttons. Admins can see exactly what it read in Spark activity. For the Ad/Spam Filter, add a real example to the example ads.
A chat isn’t in AutoTag or the Ad/Spam Filter yet.
Chats wait for their transcript, which arrives when the chat ends.
Silent close shows a ✗.
Press Silent close again…. If a field keeps failing, the Product or Issue Type option may have been renamed in Zendesk.
A number looks old.
You may be seeing the faded last view; it refreshes in a moment. The refresh button top right reads again now.
